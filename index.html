Learn Marketing with Phoebe

Marketing is a short chain of decisions that most teams make out of order: they pick channels before they know who they are for, write the promise before they know what the customer would otherwise do, and set a budget before they know what a customer is worth. Six sessions make the decisions in the right order, on one constructed product, a note-taker called Cadence, and one real funnel: 8,000 genuine leads from a Brazilian marketplace and the 842 that became paying sellers. Nothing here needs an AI tool or a line of code. It needs a sheet, a calculator, and the willingness to write down who it is not for.

The six sessions 🎯 · from who it is for to a plan someone can approve

The running product is Cadence, a fictional AI note-taker sold two ways, solo and Teams; every Cadence number is constructed and labelled. The real data is the public Marketing Funnel by Olist, every lead with the channel it came from and whether it closed.

🎯 Session 1 · foundations

Who it is for, and who it is not

Segments as groups that buy alike and talk to each other, the beachhead you win first, and an ideal customer profile with a written not-for list. Five Cadence segments scored on one sheet.

▶ Start here 🧭 Session 2 · foundations

The promise, against the real alternative

Positioning built the way April Dunford builds it: start from what customers would do if you did not exist, then the attributes only you have, the value they unlock, who cares most, and the category that makes it obvious.

five components 🗺️ Session 3 · core

Where they already are

Channel choice with the Bullseye from Traction: all nineteen channels on the table, a middle ring of cheap tests, one channel in the centre. Plus why the rule of seven touchpoints has no study behind it.

the Bullseye 🎛️ Session 4 · bench night

The funnel bench

8,000 real leads and 842 real closed deals from the public Olist funnel, computed in your browser. Split a budget across six channels and read the leads, deals, CAC and payback. Then break it on purpose.

★ the bench 💰 Session 5 · core

What a customer is worth

CAC, payback and lifetime value on a constructed Cadence Teams account, what each formula quietly assumes, and what David Skok actually wrote about the 3 to 1 ratio and the twelve-month line.

CAC, payback, LTV 📝 Session 6 · core

The one-page marketing plan

Sessions 1 to 5 on a single page someone can approve: who, the promise, the channel and its test, the budget split and its CAC, the number that says stop. And the brand-versus-activation argument nobody has settled.
